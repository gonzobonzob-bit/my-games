# Callsigns — the mogul ladder, researched first

Rule 4 pass on the roadmap radio → venue → label → film. Read with
`RESEARCH_RADIO_OPERATIONS.md` and `ROOMS_OWNER_NOTES.md`.

**Verdict up front: the ladder is wrong at stage 2 and again at stage 3.** The
venue and the label are the two adjacencies the American radio industry actually
tested at scale and then unwound. The one it kept is on-demand. Stage 2 should be
**the network** — your own show, cut for other operators' stations.

---

## 1. Do radio groups own venues, labels or film?

**Venues: tried once, at the largest possible scale, and reversed.** Clear
Channel bought SFX Entertainment for $4.4B in 2000 and renamed it Clear Channel
Entertainment. In 2004 it billed **$2.8B — nearly 30% of company revenue — on
$16.3M of net income, a 0.58% net margin** — and was spun off as Live Nation in
December 2005. The reason given by everyone involved is that the "we will promote
our concerts on our own radio stations" synergy never paid.
([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/how-corporate-merger-changed-concert-business-forever-25-years-ago/),
[Fortune/CNN](https://money.cnn.com/2007/11/30/news/companies/live_nation.fortune/index.htm),
[Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/54/Live-Nation-Inc.html))

It also produced the antitrust suit that defines the combination: *Nobody in
Particular Presents v. Clear Channel*, 311 F. Supp. 2d 1048 (D. Colo. 2004),
alleging airplay was withheld from artists who used rival promoters. Settled June
2004. ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2314571/nobody-in-part-presents-inc-v-clear-channel-communs-inc/),
[Stereophile](https://www.stereophile.com/news/060704clearchannel/index.html))

**Tried again at small scale, and reversed again.** Townsquare Media ran a real
live-events business across its markets and deliberately exited it. Digital is
now **59% of net revenue and 63% of segment profit**.
([Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/digital-growth-fills-live-event-hole-at-townsquare-media/article_a9164016-e25e-11e8-a488-5f10e6e26f34.html),
[Townsquare Q1 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/11/3291670/0/en/townsquare-reports-q1-results-with-digital-driving-63-of-segment-profit-and-59-of-net-revenue.html))

**What iHeart kept is not a venue.** The iHeartRadio Music Festival and Jingle
Ball are **sponsorship inventory** — ~10 packages worth $10–15M in collective ad
dollars, sold to the same national advertisers by the same sales organisation.
iHeart rents the T-Mobile Arena; it does not own it.
([Event Marketer](https://www.eventmarketer.com/article/iheartradio-music-festival-scaling-music-fan-and-sponsor-engagement/),
[Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/articles/business/6266454/iheartmedia-relaunch-successful-fest-uncertain-future))

**Labels: no, and the law is why.** §317 of the Communications Act and 47 CFR
73.1212 require sponsorship identification whenever a station receives anything
of value for what it airs. Four groups — Clear Channel, CBS Radio, Entercom,
Citadel — paid **$12.5M in 2007 consent decrees** over undisclosed label
arrangements. The FCC issued a fresh **Enforcement Advisory in February 2025**
warning that trading airplay for a band's appearance at a station event is
payola, and entered a **consent decree with iHeart in July 2026** on that fact
pattern. ([Broadcast Law Blog 2007](https://www.broadcastlawblog.com/2007/04/articles/fcc-issues-payola-settlement-orders-12-5-million-more-for-uncle-sam/),
[Broadcast Law Blog 2025](https://www.broadcastlawblog.com/2025/02/articles/fcc-enforcement-advisory-warns-of-payola-concerns-in-coercing-bands-to-play-at-broadcast-station-events-with-threats-of-decreased-airplay-and-reminds-all-broadcasters-radio-and-tv-of-spons/),
[Broadcast Law Blog 2026](https://www.broadcastlawblog.com/2026/07/articles/fcc-consent-decree-with-iheart-discusses-how-exchanging-airplay-for-discounts-or-free-stuff-can-trigger-sponsorship-identification-requirements-including-for-songs-played-in-exchange-for-a-band/))

So the one mechanic a designer would reach for — "my stations promote my venue's
shows, my stations play my label's records" — is the exact conduct the FCC
polices. Done lawfully (disclosed as sponsored) it loses its promotional value.

**What they actually built and kept: on-demand.** iHeart FY2025 Digital Audio
**$1.329B, +14.2%; podcast $563.7M, +25.6%; 34–38% segment EBITDA margin**,
against Multiplatform (broadcast) $2.274B and **falling 4.2%**.
([iHeart FY2025 results](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/iheartmedia-inc-reports-results-2025-210000407.html),
[Radio World](https://www.radioworld.com/news-and-business/business-and-law/iheartmedia-says-revenue-grew-slightly-in-2025))

## 2. The venue business, briefly, so the rejection is informed

Club/theatre scale: artist takes a **guarantee or ~80/20 of the net door**,
whichever is greater; the venue's 20% is meant to cover house sound, door staff
and the show's advertising. Bar and concessions alone do not cover operating
cost at that scale. **64% of US independent venues were unprofitable in 2024.**
([Ticket Fairy](https://www.ticketfairy.com/blog/deal-or-no-deal-navigating-booking-contracts-settlements-for-venues-in-2026),
[443 Social Club](https://443socialclub.com/economics-of-live-music-in-small-venue-revisited/),
[NIVA via Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/cleveland/2025/10/16/indie-music-venues-profitability-niva-2025))

Staff: talent buyer, venue/GM, production manager, house sound engineer, box
office, bar, security. ([Berklee — talent buyer](https://www.berklee.edu/careers/roles/talent-buyer),
[Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/types-of-jobs-at-music-venue))

**Which of those overlap a radio operation: sales and promotions only.** The
engineer does not. SBE certification (CBRE/CBTE, AM directional, RF safety) is
transmitter, STL and emission-mask work; live production is IATSE
stagehand/live-sound — a different trade, different training, different union.
([SBE](https://sbe.org/certification/sbe-certification-program/),
[Stagehand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagehand))

**So the pitch "the same engineer who tends your Class C transmitter competes to
rig the stage" is a fantasy, and it should be said plainly.** It is the most
appealing sentence in the brief and it has no real counterpart.

## 3. The label business, briefly

Signing through release costs **$70k–$550k per artist**; recoupment takes
**1–3 years**; third-party distribution takes **15–30%** of revenue; streaming
pays around **$0.003/stream**. ([BusinessDojo](https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/record-label-profitability),
[Soundcharts](https://soundcharts.com/en/blog/splits-and-profits-record-deals-analysis))

A 1–3 year lag is 365–1,095 in-game days against a run that ends around day 540.
A label is a mechanic whose feedback arrives after the run is over.

## 4. What actually constrains an operator running more than one of these

Not attention. **Capital structure.** iHeartMedia filed Chapter 11 in 2018 with
**over $20B of debt** from the 2008 LBO; Cumulus filed in 2017; Audacy filed in
January 2024 and eliminated ~$1.6B of $1.9B funded debt. Every major US radio
group has been through it, all from acquisition leverage.
([Variety](https://variety.com/2019/music/news/iheartmedia-bankruptcy-plan-approval-1203114664/),
[Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/01/07/radio-giant-audacy-files-for-bankruptcy))

**Design consequence, and it is the most important one in this document:** a
stage you buy with cash imports the real industry's actual failure mode, which
Callsigns does not model (there is no debt in the game). A stage you buy with
**person-hours** attaches to the spine that already exists. Stage 2 must cost
assignments, not a purchase button.

## 5. Stage 2 — THE NETWORK

Real counterpart: barter syndication. A syndicator gives the show to affiliate
stations for **no cash**; the affiliate gives up commercial positions inside the
show ("national avails"), which the syndicator sells. Value is **clearances** —
how many stations carry you, weighted by their markets. Premiere Networks,
Westwood One, Compass Media Networks all run this.
([Broadcast syndication](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/social-sciences-and-humanities/broadcast-syndication),
[Intrac](https://www.intracglobal.com/bartersyndication))

It has the property the venue was wanted for and does not pay for: **a network
feed has no daypart.** It is produced once, in the building, and airs on someone
else's clock.

### 5.1 Objects, each with its real-world counterpart

| Object | Real thing |
|---|---|
| **Feed** | one barter-syndicated show the group originates (Premiere's national shows) |
| **Cut** (episode) | the produced hour delivered to affiliates each day |
| **Feed seat** | the producer/board op who cuts it; **an assignment in `staffSlotLoad()`**, exactly like a room seat |
| **Affiliate** | Sunbelt Media / Lantern Group / Ridgeway Family clearing your show in a segment they operate in |
| **Clearance** | the industry's own metric: affiliates weighted by market audience |
| **Barter split** | the avails inside the hour the syndicator keeps; the affiliate pays no cash |
| **Distribution cut** | the distributor/rep fee, 15–30% real, `DIST_CUT = 0.20` |
| **Freshness** | on-demand decay: ~80% of listening inside 48h, evergreen first-7-days is 40–50% of lifetime ([Edison via B2B benchmarks](https://shareyourgenius.com/b2b-podcast-benchmarks-what-the-data-really-means-for-your-show/)) |

No object here needed inventing. There is no "bay".

### 5.2 The core decision, one sentence

**Each day, for each of your best hosts: leave them live on their daypart, or
voice-track that daypart and spend the freed person-hours cutting the network
feed — knowing which rivals will carry it depends on how big those rivals
currently are, which moves on a 34-day cycle you cannot rush.**

### 5.3 The value formula

```
netRev = NET_VALUE · fresh · (1 − DIST_CUT) · Σ_{net ∈ carried} affiliateAud(seg, part, net)

affiliateAud = segPop(seg, part) · k_net / ( Σ_j k_j + ownPull(seg, part) )

carried(net)  iff  feedQuality ≥ CARRY_PER_K · k_net
fresh         decays daily, reset to 1 by a cut; exactly 0 with no seated staff
```

`k_net` is `rivalK(segId, netId)` — the persistent capacity v5 already ships.
`ownPull`, `segPop`, `rivalPull` are existing functions. Nothing new is invented
in the engine; the feed reads the rival table the game already maintains.

### 5.4 The self-reference check, shown as working

Every input to `netRev`, and whether the player sets it **in order to** be paid
by this mechanic:

| Input | Player-set? | In order to benefit here? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `segPop(seg, part)` | no | — | world constant, from `SEGMENTS` |
| `NET_VALUE`, `DIST_CUT`, `CARRY_PER_K` | no | — | world constants, sourced in §1/§3 |
| `k_net` (rival capacity) | **influenced** | **they would want it LOW, and the mechanic pays MORE when it is low** | **safe, and signed against the exploit** |
| `ownPull` | yes | it sits in the **denominator** — raising it for local revenue *reduces* feed revenue | opposed, not aligned |
| `fresh` | yes | it is a **cost** input capped at 1, not a rate; a second seat cannot push it above 1 | bounded |
| `feedQuality` | yes | it is the product being sold; it gates carriage but does not scale payment | gate, not multiplier |
| cash, payroll, revenue, schedule shape | **absent** | — | none appear anywhere in `netRev` |

The line that matters is `k_net`. The obvious attack is "abandon a market so its
rivals compound, then sell them a show". `tickRivalCapacity()` grows `k` when
held share is under `RIVAL_TARGET = 0.032`. But growing `k` **raises**
`CARRY_PER_K · k_net`, so the affiliate you grew stops taking your show; and
`affiliateAud` is self-normalising because `k_net` appears in its own denominator
(`Σk + ownPull`), so a market where every rival doubled delivers barely more
audience per affiliate than one where they halved. Farming `k` costs real share
and returns less carriage. **The exploit is negative-signed, and §6 measures it.**

Compare the two designs this check has now failed. `newsMul()` was ceilinged by
`servedSlots(st, ROOM_NEWS)` — a count of dayparts the player chose knowing the
room existed. Purr & Power's market price was derived from the player's own cost
basis. `netRev`'s ceiling is `Σ segPop · k/(Σk + ownPull)`: it contains no count
of anything the player scheduled and no term derived from anything they paid.

### 5.5 What this does NOT use, deliberately

**The `medium` tag stays unused.** It is a runway for a second kind of *licensed
station* — a TV row with `pop`, `comp`, `leaseMul`, `fit`. A feed has no market,
no population, no lease and no signal. Giving it a `SEGMENTS` row to make use of
an available join point is the same backwards derivation that produced bays.
The join point remains correctly unused until something is licensed into a
market.

*(Integrator's note: `js/content.js:1168` currently describes the `medium` tag as
"what makes a future venue or …" possible. If the network is adopted, that
comment should be corrected — it is the last place in the codebase still
pointing at the rejected roadmap.)*

## 6. One worked ordering that flips

One host, skill 5, on `citywide` morning drive. `citywide`: `pop.morning = 6000`,
`comp.base = 2000`, nets w = .45/.35/.20, so opening k = 900/700/400.
Revenue per listener-day at rep 40 = `adRate 1.10 · AD_VALUE 0.42 · fill 0.71 ·
price 1.15 · repRevMul 1.286` = **$0.485**. Tracking costs `TRACK_APPEAL = 0.88`
on pull. `NET_VALUE = 0.030`, `CARRY_PER_K = 1/900`, feed quality 0.80.

**Option A** — host stays live, no feed. **Option B** — track the daypart
(`0.35` load) and seat the host on the feed.

**State 1: you have held `citywide` at 20% for ~150 days.**
`drift = clamp(1 − 0.20/0.032, −1.5, 1) = −1.5`, so `k` falls 0.9%/day into the
`RIVAL_K_MIN` floor: Σk = 600 (270 / 210 / 120).
- Your pull p = 150 (share 150/750 = 0.20 → 1,200 listeners).
- Tracked: p' = 132 → 132/732 = 0.1803 → 1,082. **Loss = 118 · $0.485 = $57.2/day.**
- Carriage thresholds 0.300 / 0.233 / 0.133 — quality 0.80 clears **all three**.
- Affiliate audience = 6000 · 600/750 = 4,800 → `netRev` = 4,800 · 0.030 · 0.80
  (dist cut) = **$115.2/day** net of the 20% distribution cut → **B wins by $58.0/day.**

**State 2: you moved to `nightshift` and held `citywide` at 1% for ~150 days.**
`drift = 1 − 0.01/0.032 = +0.6875` → `k · (1.00412)^150 = 1.86x` → Σk = 3,720
(1,674 / 1,302 / 744).
- Your pull p = 37.6 → 60 listeners. Tracked → 52.9. **Loss = $3.4/day.**
- Carriage thresholds 1.860 / 1.447 / 0.827 — quality 0.80 clears **none**.
- `netRev` = **$0** → **A wins.**

**State 3: equilibrium, held share 0.032, k at opening.**
Thresholds 1.000 / 0.778 / 0.444 → clears Lantern and Ridgeway, not Sunbelt.
Affiliate audience = 6000 · 1,100/2,066 = 3,194 → **$76.7/day** net, against a
loss of 23 listeners = $11.2/day → **B wins by $65.5/day**, but with a different
affiliate set, so the feed's revenue changes without the player touching it.

The ordering flips on rival capacity, which the player moves only by paying its
full price in surrendered local share, on a clock that takes ~116 days to double.
"Always feed" and "never feed" are both wrong, and the crossing point moves.

## 7. `LOSABLE` — two structural zeros, no tuning

Idle currently dies day 369. The feed cannot rescue it, for reasons that are
exact rather than balanced:

1. **`fresh = 0` with no seated staff.** An idle run seats nobody, so `netRev`
   multiplies by an exact zero. Same shape as `roomPts = 0` and `trackedOn =
   false`: the no-feed path is bit-identical to today.
2. **Idle grows its own rivals out of reach.** Held share → 0 gives
   `drift = +1`, `k → 2.2 · open` (`RIVAL_K_MAX`) in ~130 days. Carriage requires
   `feedQuality ≥ CARRY_PER_K · k`, i.e. ≥ 2.2 for the largest net — unreachable
   without hiring and training, which an idle run does not do.

**Additional non-negotiables for the build:**
- A feed seat adds `+1` to `staffSlotLoad()` and contributes **nothing** to
  `stationAttn()`. Feeds cost attention; they never make it. Same rule rooms obey.
- `netRev` must not touch `slotPull()` or `avgQuality`. Reputation stays out of
  the feed path, or the spiral guard is defeated.
- `CARRY_PER_K · k_net` must be evaluated against the **absolute** `k`, not
  `k/open`. Against the ratio, all three nets flip together and the segment
  becomes one binary instead of three.

**The risk this design carries, which the harness must measure:** it pays the
operator who is already dominant, so it could widen a runaway lead. The
counterweight is the `RIVAL_K_MIN = 0.30` floor, which caps how much affiliate
audience a crushed market can deliver. Balance must confirm that cap binds before
`greedy` outruns `careful` by more than it does today.

## 8. Pressure curve, additions only

- **Minute 5** — untouched to the dime. Feed is locked behind rep and a first
  affiliate pitch; day one is `$60/day` lease against `$60.9` gross, unchanged.
- **Hour 1** — nothing. The feed does not exist yet; this is deliberate.
- **Hour 10** — the **34-day carriage cycle** (`RIVAL_PERIOD`). Every cycle each
  affiliate re-decides. A market you crushed pays you as an affiliate; the day you
  stop crushing it, it grows and drops you. That is a clock pulling **against**
  expansion, which the game currently has none of.

**Scarce resource: person-hours, unchanged.** The only way to afford a
high-skill feed seat is to voice-track a daypart at `0.35` and eat `TRACK_APPEAL
= 0.88`. The feed does not invent a resource; it makes the existing one hurt in a
second direction.

**Failure state: unchanged.** Bankruptcy. `netRev` adds no floor — it is
multiplied by a freshness term that decays to zero the moment the seat is empty.

## 9. What NOT to build

- **A theatre.** The single most disproven combination in the industry's history:
  $2.8B revenue, $16.3M net income, spun off; Townsquare exited the same business
  at small scale; 64% of independent venues unprofitable.
- **A label.** 1–3 year recoupment against a ~540-day run, and a broadcaster
  owning one turns its own airplay into a sponsorship-identification problem.
- **Film.** No radio group has ever done it. There is nothing to research.
- **Anything bought with a cash button.** The real constraint on multi-business
  radio operators is leverage, which Callsigns does not model. Buy stages with
  assignments.

**One optional survivor, not this pass:** a **Live Room** — a fourth room type
with a real counterpart (KEXP's Live Room, WXPN/World Café Live) where an act
cuts a session that refreshes the feed. It is a room, not a stage; it costs a
seat; and if it is ever built, the §317 exposure from trading airplay for that
session is an **event**, not a synergy.
